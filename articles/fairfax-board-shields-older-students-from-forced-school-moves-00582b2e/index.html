Fairfax Board Shields Older Students From Forced School Moves

By Tysons Times Staff

Fairfax

Schools
Published
July 15, 2025
Topics
schools, fairfax, local government

Article Text

Fairfax County students already enrolled in middle or high school will generally be able to remain at their current school when boundary changes take effect, under a revised phase-in policy unanimously approved by the school board Thursday, July 17.

The change comes as Fairfax County Public Schools moves through a division-wide redistricting review that has prompted petitions, demonstrations and other strong reactions to draft scenarios released by a consultant in May.

Under the revised policy, incoming students must attend the school assigned under the new boundaries. Most students already attending a middle, high or secondary school may choose either to stay or transfer to their newly assigned school.

At middle schools serving grades 6-8, the new boundary will be mandatory for incoming sixth graders. At schools serving grades 7-8, it will apply to incoming seventh graders. Other enrolled students may remain at their current school, except when a school is opening or closing.

Incoming ninth graders must follow the new boundaries at high schools. Students entering grades 10-12 may stay at their existing school or attend the newly assigned one. Those options also will not apply when a school opens or closes.

At secondary schools, incoming seventh graders must attend the school assigned under the new boundary, while rising eighth through 12th graders may choose between the two schools.

Elementary students entering their school’s final two grades—either fourth and fifth or fifth and sixth—will also have the option to remain or follow the new boundary.

Mason District Representative Ricardy Anderson led the effort to revise the policy. She argued that the previous approach could unnecessarily disrupt older high school students and middle schoolers, who often spend only two years at their school before advancing.

“I’m very concerned that the boundary policy, as it stands, only allows for those who are rising seniors to not be impacted by boundary adjustments,” Anderson said before the vote. She noted that rising sophomores and juniors could otherwise be moved after completing part of their high school experience.

The board’s previous policy generally allowed members to determine phase-in arrangements separately for each boundary study. It suggested—but did not require—giving students entering their final year at a school the option to remain.

That policy was adopted in July 2024 as part of an effort to standardize a boundary process that FCPS had traditionally initiated in response to crowding at individual schools or requests from board members. The updated framework also requires the superintendent to examine boundaries across the school division every five years.

During the July 18, 2024, vote, Anderson and Hunter Mill District Representative Melanie Meren supported allowing all current middle school students, high school students in grades 10-12 and elementary students in their final two years to choose whether to move.

The board instead voted 7-4 for the case-by-case approach proposed by At-Large Representative Kyle McDaniel, who said specifying grade levels could create logistical difficulties.

A year into the boundary review, Anderson said families are more aware of how the changes could affect them.

“Life’s events will change things, but we shouldn’t have to compel a change,” she said. “We should put a stake in the ground that we are committed to having these students have a consistent educational experience at those levels.”

A survey collecting feedback on the draft boundary proposals is scheduled to close Friday, July 18, at 5 p.m.
