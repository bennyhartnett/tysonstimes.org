Planning Commission backs long-term redevelopment at Reston Town Center North

The Fairfax County Planning Commission unanimously recommended zoning and land-use changes July 22 that would establish a framework for the phased redevelopment of Reston Town Center North.

By Tysons Times Staff

Coverage area
Reston
Section
Civic Desk
Published
July 27, 2026
Topics
civic, reston, development, schools

Article Text

The Fairfax County Planning Commission unanimously recommended zoning and land-use changes July 22 that would establish a framework for the phased redevelopment of Reston Town Center North.

The advisory vote was not final project approval. As of July 27, the rezoning application was scheduled for a Fairfax County Board of Supervisors public hearing on Sept. 15, 2026. Each parcel would still undergo a separate site-review process before final action by the Board of Supervisors.

The conceptual plan covers nearly 47.9 acres north of the existing Reston Town Center, bounded by Baron Cameron Avenue, Fountain Drive, Town Center Parkway and New Dominion Parkway. Bowman Towne Drive divides the site internally.

Through a joint venture between Fairfax County and Inova, the framework allows an estimated 1.6 million square feet of redevelopment. It anticipates approximately 1,000 housing units, mixed-use properties, athletic fields and new public facilities, including a Reston Regional Library, a replacement Embry Rucker Shelter and a new human-services building.

The plan also includes a 3.6-acre central green. Initial improvements are expected during the first phase, which is also expected to include the replacement library and shelter. County staff projected that the new library would open in 2030, with the replacement shelter following several months later. Existing facilities are expected to remain open until their replacements are completed.

The library and shelter projects have been funded through bond referendums, according to the project presentation reported by FFXnow. Those projected opening dates, like the broader schedule, are not guarantees. The buildout is expected to continue into the 2050s, leaving future uses, designs and timing subject to change.

Most of the site’s 11 blocks are expected to be redeveloped in phases. Conceptual-plan proffers establish limits on maximum uses, building height and density for individual blocks. Approval of the conceptual plans would also enable Fairfax County and Inova to undertake land swaps that may be needed for future development.

A 4.4-acre parcel in the site’s northwest area, now occupied by the North County Human Services Center, is reserved for possible future Fairfax County Public Schools use, potentially as an elementary school. That use has not been finalized.

The area already includes an Inova emergency-care facility and the Sunrise at Reston Town Center senior-living facility. The 38,000-square-foot North County Governmental Center, which opened in 2016 at 1801 Cameron Glen Drive, is expected to remain while redevelopment proceeds around it.

The proposal has drawn both support and requests for additional assurances. Reston Association expressed general support while seeking commitments related to affordable and workforce housing, natural turf, assisted living, energy-efficiency standards and timely completion of the central green.

Peter Lusk said the green should be delivered promptly as a community benefit. “The central green is an important community amenity, and it should be completed in a timely manner for residents to enjoy,” he said. Lusk also said the project “should serve as a model for environmental stewardship throughout the county.”

Nearby resident Evan Freund described the plan favorably but asked officials to prioritize pedestrian safety. Phil Niedzielski-Eichner acknowledged the proposal’s difficult history, saying, “It has not been without controversy.”

Mary Cortina said the proposal had received extensive community review. “This plan has been really thoroughly vetted with the community from the moment that Supervisor [Walter] Alcorn came on board,” she said.

Planning Commission Chairman Timothy Sampson emphasized that the vote marked a starting point for future decisions, not the end of the process. “What we have set up is a framework. We’re going to be back here, and our successors are going to be back here, over time,” he said. “The proffers set guardrails for what’s going to come next.”

The site has been part of Fairfax County’s long-range planning for years. Gulf Reston, successor to Reston founder Robert E. Simon, deeded the property to the county in the 1970s. A 2010 update to the Reston Comprehensive Plan first envisioned redevelopment, and a 2023 task force recommended reserving the human-services-center parcel for possible future school use.

Commissioner John Carter’s July 22 vote was his last after nine years on the commission.
