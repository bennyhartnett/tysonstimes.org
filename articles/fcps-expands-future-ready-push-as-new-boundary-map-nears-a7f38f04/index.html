FCPS Expands Future-Ready Push as New Boundary Map Nears

Article Text

Fairfax County Public Schools launched the third year of its Lighthouse Schools program last week, advancing an effort to test new teaching strategies and technology before sharing successful approaches across the division.

The kickoff took place Monday at South County Middle School. The program is designed to help students and employees build skills for a changing workplace while encouraging schools to develop solutions tailored to their communities.

During the program’s first two years, Lemon Road Elementary examined equity in family engagement, using journey mapping and new feedback methods to better understand families’ experiences. Centre Ridge Elementary introduced technology-based career portfolios to promote career exploration and student confidence.

McLean High School redesigned Advanced Placement courses, created externship opportunities and transformed laboratory spaces to make learning more active and relevant.

Later Monday, student representatives from every FCPS high school gathered at Woodson High School for the annual kickoff of the Superintendent’s Student Advisory Council. Members discussed initiatives for the 2025-26 school year and received updates on the division’s response to recommendations developed during the previous school year.

Those recommendations addressed attendance, literacy, multilingual learner programs, inclusion, access to extracurricular activities, and college and career planning. The council’s work supports Goal 2 of the FCPS 2023-30 Strategic Plan, which focuses on ensuring students feel safe, supported, included and empowered.

Boundary review moves toward a fourth scenario

The Superintendent’s Boundary Review Advisory Committee met Wednesday as FCPS continued its review of school attendance boundaries.

During the spring, community members responded to three draft scenarios focused on neighborhood connectivity and attendance islands, cohort continuity and split feeder patterns, and overcrowding through comprehensive balance.

A fourth scenario is now being developed from those earlier proposals. It incorporates priorities shaped by the advisory committee after its review of community feedback and is being prepared by the division’s consultant.

The new draft map is expected to be presented for public feedback during October community meetings and added to the Boundary Explorer Tool. Maps available in the tool at the time of the update remained the versions shown during the spring meetings. Two additional September community meetings were scheduled for Sept. 29 and 30.

$460 million school bond heads to voters

FCPS also outlined its strategic priorities and funding challenges Thursday during a meeting with the Tysons Rotary Club at the Tower Club in Vienna.

The discussion included the 2025 School Bond Referendum. Fairfax County voters will decide Nov. 4 whether to approve $460 million for school renovations, construction, planning and design.

Renovations at Wakefield Forest Elementary were completed and celebrated with a ribbon-cutting about a month earlier, while several other projects remain underway across the county. FCPS facilities also accommodate roughly 500,000 community events each year, including meetings and activities for homeowners associations, Scout groups, civic organizations and athletic programs.

At Thursday’s School Board meeting, FCPS presented its approach to developing “future-ready” students under Goal 5 of the strategic plan, Leading for Tomorrow’s Innovation.

The division’s model calls for students to build durable skills, understand and communicate their strengths, advocate for themselves, make informed life and financial decisions, and participate in learning that creates future opportunities.

Elementary students encounter workplace concepts through simulated classroom jobs, applications and résumés. Middle school students practice budgeting and saving at Finance Park, while high school students meet local employers through the Instruction to Industry: A Future Ready Conference, where interactions can lead to internships, employment and other career opportunities.

The School Board also received the annual Opening of Schools Report, reviewing the first day of classes and previewing the 2025-26 school year.

Other updates included the start of fall sports and the division’s new girls flag football program, along with recent back-to-school nights, school and library visits, Community and Employee Conversations, and the fourth annual Educate Fairfax Alumni Hall of Fame.

Festivals, robotics and marching bands fill the weekend

Woodburn Elementary held its first Hispanic Heritage Festival on Friday evening as part of National Hispanic Heritage Month. Students, families and employees gathered for music, food, crafts and other activities.

Later that night, West Springfield High School hosted South County for varsity football and cheer.

On Saturday morning, 32 teams from across the region competed in the Chesapeake Robotics Icebreaker at Hayfield Secondary School. The offseason event allowed teams to test their robots, fix problems, gain drive-team experience and practice under competition conditions before the regular season.

Hayfield’s Night Hawks were among the participants. The school has hosted five FIRST Robotics Competition events since 2018, including Saturday’s contest. FIRST Robotics Competition was established in 1989, and Herndon High School has operated the longest-running FCPS team since 1996.

The event gave students practical experience not only in science, technology, engineering and mathematics, but also in teamwork, communication and critical thinking.

FCPS employees and local families also met Saturday at the Parents of Autistic Children of Northern Virginia’s annual Back to School meeting in Springfield. The discussion covered literacy differentiation, inclusion, staffing and budget developments, with participation from the FCPS Department of Special Services.

The week concluded at Herndon High School with the 2025 Showcase of Bands. More than 30 high school marching bands from around the region assembled to open the competition season. Herndon has hosted the annual event since 1998.
