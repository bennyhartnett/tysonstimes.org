From 16 Tables to a Northern Virginia Dining Empire

Article Text

Great American Restaurants has grown from a small Fairfax City pizza shop into a family-owned hospitality group with nearly 20 restaurants and three artisan bakeries across Virginia and Maryland.

Randy Norton and Jim Farley founded the business in 1974, with Mike Ranney joining soon afterward. Its first restaurant had just 16 tables. In 1976, the team opened Fantastic Fritzbe’s Flying Food Factory, the concept now known as Silverado, helping launch the broader restaurant group.

More than 50 years later, the Fairfax-based company operates a varied collection of neighborhood dining concepts throughout Northern Virginia and the wider Washington region.

A Restaurant for Nearly Every Occasion

The group’s portfolio spans seafood, steaks, burgers, barbecue, tavern food and other American standards.

Coastal Flats, which focuses on seafood and casual dining, has locations in Fairfax, Tysons Corner and Gaithersburg, Maryland. Sweetwater Tavern serves tavern fare and house beers in Centreville, Merrifield and Sterling.

Patsy’s American brings together popular dishes from the company’s history, while Best Buns Bakery & Burgers combines baked goods with burgers and brunch. Jackson’s Mighty Fine Food & Lucky Lounge offers a bistro and bar setting in Reston.

The company also operates Artie’s, Mike’s American Grill, Ozzie’s Good Eats, Carlyle, Randy’s Prime Seafood & Steaks, Silverado and Stupid Good BBQ. Together, the concepts cover everything from family meals and casual gatherings to dates and celebrations, with an emphasis on food prepared from scratch.

Training and Long-Term Careers

Founder and Chairman Randy Norton helped shape the company’s focus on neighborhood hospitality and employee involvement. CEO Jon Norton has continued emphasizing staff support, training and professional growth.

Employees across the restaurants and bakeries participate in a structured training program intended to maintain consistent food and service. The company also offers management development and benefits designed to encourage longer-term careers.

Its workplace practices have earned Great American Restaurants recognition among The Washington Post’s Top Workplaces, although online employee discussions offer differing views of its strict training and uniform requirements. Some workers value that structure, while others find it demanding.

Desserts Help Fund Local Causes

The company directs 25 cents from every dessert sale to charities including the Arlington Food Assistance Center, Food For Others, No Kid Hungry, So Others Might Eat and the Latino Student Fund.

Best Buns bakeries also donate a portion of bread sales to charitable causes. A Great American Restaurants scholarship at George Mason University supports local students pursuing higher education.

The group expanded its hunger-relief work during the COVID-19 pandemic by establishing the nonprofit Great American Community Kitchen. Its food trucks prepare and distribute free hot meals throughout Northern Virginia.

The initiative has served millions of meals since it began and works with organizations such as FACETS to reach people in local neighborhoods.

Loyal Customers—and Some Criticism

Many diners describe Great American Restaurants locations as community fixtures, praising their familiar atmosphere, comfort food and service. Sweetwater Tavern and Coastal Flats have particularly strong local followings, while Ozzie rolls and seafood dishes are frequently singled out by customers.

Reviews are not universally glowing. Some customers consider parts of the menus dated or expensive for the value, and others report uneven service. Still, longtime employees and well-established locations continue to attract loyal patrons.

From its 16-table beginning to its current regional footprint, Great American Restaurants has become a prominent part of Northern Virginia’s dining landscape. Its restaurants remain the most visible side of the business, but its scholarships, charitable donations and free-meal program have extended its reach well beyond the dining room.
