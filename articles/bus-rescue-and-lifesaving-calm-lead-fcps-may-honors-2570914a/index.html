Bus Rescue and Lifesaving Calm Lead FCPS May Honors

Article Text

Fairfax County Public Schools recognized employees across the division in its May 2026 FCPS CARES roundup, highlighting acts ranging from an unusual bus rescue in Washington, D.C., to emergency assistance during a choking incident.

May’s regional honorees

Central Office recognition went to Ferdinando Greco and Mervin Greenwood of Stonecroft Transportation. On May 1, a field-trip bus broke down in a traffic lane in Washington during a protest. Police prevented a tow truck from reaching it, leaving the driver stranded for hours. After dispatch requested help late Friday afternoon, supervisor Greenwood drove Greco into the city. Greco relieved the stranded driver and waited until the bus was towed, while Greenwood returned the original driver to his parking location.

Callie Harris of Cooper Middle School was honored in Region 1 for transforming a seventh-grader’s relationship with science and providing extensive support during the student’s medical leave. Harris sent encouraging messages, visited the student at home to proctor a test and became certified as a homebound teacher so she could continue providing instruction.

Region 2 recognized Patti Harling-Gray of Haycock Elementary School for mentoring a first-year teacher developing an Algebra 1 Honors pilot curriculum. She was also praised for challenging younger students with advanced concepts, including multistep equations involving the distributive property.

Grace Vivirito of Whitman Middle School received the Region 3 honor for expanding participation in the Early Identification Program. Whitman recorded the most nominations among EIP schools and led in completed applications after Vivirito organized support sessions for students and families.

Region 4 honored Ranilo Abrigo of Robinson Secondary School. During a Teacher Appreciation luncheon, Abrigo calmly assisted a choking colleague, prepared to perform the Heimlich maneuver and guided her until she cleared the obstruction. The former Air Force medic then helped her regulate her breathing and remain seated as she recovered.

Betty Ablimit of Chantilly High School was recognized in Region 5 for supporting the local Uyghur community. Since 2023, she has led the Capital Uyghur Youth Sports Club, coached teenage girls in volleyball on Fridays, assisted student clubs and tutored young people. She has also helped parents understand special-education resources and their children’s IEPs.

Region 6 recognized Rachael Altekruse of Lewis High School for sustaining a challenging, high-quality orchestra program despite limited funding and students’ uneven access to private lessons.
