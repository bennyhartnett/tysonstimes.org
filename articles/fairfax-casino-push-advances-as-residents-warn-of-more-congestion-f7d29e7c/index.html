Fairfax Casino Push Advances as Residents Warn of More Congestion

Fairfax County voters could eventually decide whether to allow a casino after legislation authorizing a referendum advanced to Virginia’s House of Delegates.

By Tysons Times Staff

Coverage area
Fairfax
Section
Business
Published
February 17, 2026
Topics
business, fairfax, transportation

Article Text

Fairfax County voters could eventually decide whether to allow a casino after legislation authorizing a referendum advanced to Virginia’s House of Delegates.

The state Senate approved the measure Friday in a 23-14 vote, despite opposition from several Northern Virginia lawmakers. Unlike earlier proposals centered on Tysons, the latest version would permit a casino anywhere in Fairfax County.

That broader scope has not eased concerns among some Tysons residents.

“It’s already congested enough,” Kristin Harrig said. “There’s enough to do here. We don’t need to gamble here. You can go across the river to the National Harbor.”

Fairfax County’s Board of Supervisors voted 5-4 against including the casino proposal among its legislative priorities. Other local elected officials have also opposed the project.

Jamaal French said he could support a Northern Virginia casino, but not in Fairfax County.

“Maybe somewhere out in Loudoun County or something like that, but not in Fairfax County,” French said. “But in Northern Virginia, definitely.”

Kevin Ejtemai, a business owner at Tysons Corner Center and father of three young children, expressed concern that a nearby casino could bring more intoxicated visitors into the surrounding community.

Supporters argue those fears are exaggerated. Senate Majority Leader Scott Surovell said organized opposition is largely confined to McLean and noted that living near MGM National Harbor has not harmed his quality of life.

“A lot of people, I think, are sort of saying the sky is going to fall, but I don’t think it’s consistent with reality,” Surovell said.

Tysons resident Dipen Patel supports bringing a casino to Fairfax County. He argued that Virginia is losing gambling revenue as residents travel to Maryland.

“As much money is flowing from Virginia to Maryland, we’re better off doing one here,” Patel said, pointing to the number of Virginia visitors traveling to MGM to gamble.

The House of Delegates gave the proposal little attention last year, according to Surovell. This year, however, he believes delegates are interested in finding a path forward and that a bill could reach the governor’s desk.

French remained opposed to placing a casino nearby, citing Fairfax County’s existing traffic problems. Still, he said he would welcome one close enough to visit—just not in his home county.
