Kings Park Library Launches Yearlong Learning Push for Older Adults

Kings Park Library is expanding opportunities for older adults in 2026 with a yearlong series shaped by requests for more programming aimed at seniors and retirees.

By Tysons Times Staff

Fairfax
Section

Published
February 27, 2026
Topics
culture, fairfax, business, arts

Article Text

Kings Park Library is expanding opportunities for older adults in 2026 with a yearlong series shaped by requests for more programming aimed at seniors and retirees.

Created by the branch’s Adult Services staff, the Lifelong Learning Series combines educational programs, practical resources and social opportunities for Fairfax County’s older adult community.

Library staff will work with local organizations to offer sessions on safe driving, scam prevention, caregiving and library resources for older adults. Participating partners include AARP, the Osher Lifelong Learning Institute at George Mason University, Fairfax County agencies, businesses and nonprofit groups.

Kings Park Library also holds recurring social and wellness activities, including music bingo, speed friending and chair yoga.

Learning Across Fairfax County

Other Fairfax County Public Library branches offer programs for older adults throughout the year. Activities range from author talks, yoga and craft workshops to musical performances, cultural events and tax assistance provided with AARP. Some sessions are available virtually.

The library system also regularly partners with the Osher Lifelong Learning Institute to present free educational programs online and at branches across the county.

Free Online Courses and Language Lessons

Library cardholders can use several online learning platforms at no charge.

LinkedIn Learning provides thousands of courses, while LearningExpress Library offers video instruction covering popular software and basic computer skills. Universal Class includes more than 500 courses, with over 55 focused on computers and technology.

Mango Languages provides conversational instruction in more than 70 languages, including English for speakers of other languages.

Maker Labs and Technology Help

Do-it-yourself Maker Labs are available at Herndon Fortnightly Library, Pohick Regional Library and Sherwood Regional Library. Visitors can use equipment such as jewelry-making tools, sewing machines and button makers for free.

The labs and other branches also offer classes in skills such as knitting and robot programming.

Many branches provide individual technology assistance for people learning to use phones, tablets or e-readers, as well as help navigating the internet. Librarians at information desks can also assist with research and recommend reputable books and other resources.
