FCPS Walkout Threats Put Student Speech Rights at Risk

Article Text

Centreville High School students who walked out to protest recent actions by Immigration and Customs Enforcement were reportedly warned by administrators that participating could jeopardize their ability to attend prom or graduation.

Such threats are unacceptable and could set a troubling precedent across Fairfax County Public Schools, including at McLean, where student walkouts have already faced tighter limits. District policies identify specific consequences for unauthorized demonstrations, with the strictest extending to brief suspensions. Threatening far harsher penalties appears disproportionate and risks suppressing student expression.

Students do not lose their First Amendment rights when they enter school. Schools may regulate speech to prevent substantial disruption to learning, but restrictions should not target views administrators dislike. Punishing students excessively for peaceful protest undermines their ability to express themselves meaningfully.

FCPS has made organizing demonstrations more difficult through an update to Regulation 2612.9, which governs student freedom of expression and prohibits walkouts during the school day. Students who communicate their plans clearly with administrators should have a path to hold an orderly daytime walkout without facing extreme consequences.

The restrictions also appear inconsistent with FCPS attendance rules, which allow an excused absence for a day of civic-engagement activities. Centreville students who gave advance notice of their participation were nevertheless marked absent without an excuse. Student demonstrations should reasonably qualify as civic engagement because they allow young people to petition the government and exercise their constitutional rights.

Confusing policies deepen the problem. The rules governing walkouts are contained in Regulation 2612.9, a document that is difficult for students to locate. Even when found, it does not clearly explain the consequences of joining an unapproved walkout.

The regulation points students to the Student Rights and Responsibilities handbook for disciplinary measures, while that handbook refers them back to Regulation 2612.9. This circular structure leaves students without a straightforward explanation of what penalties they could face.

FCPS has also missed opportunities to clarify the rules. During the mandatory Student Rights and Responsibilities update on March 10, students received a short video describing acceptable speech in broad terms and a five-question quiz. Regulation 2612.9 and its specific requirements were not clearly presented.

Students deserve an accessible summary of the policies governing their speech and the consequences of violating them. FCPS should identify Regulation 2612.9 directly, explain it in plain language and align its walkout rules with the district’s civic-engagement absence policy.

A respectful walkout protesting federal action is an act of civic participation. Schools should help students become responsible, engaged citizens—not leave them confused about their rights or discourage them with disproportionate threats.
