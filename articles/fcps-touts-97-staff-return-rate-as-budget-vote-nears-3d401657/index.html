FCPS Touts 97% Staff Return Rate as Budget Vote Nears

Fairfax County Public Schools is heading into a pivotal budget vote with encouraging news about employee retention, student innovation and community support across the division.

By Tysons Times Staff

Coverage area
Herndon
Section
Schools
Published
February 23, 2026
Topics
schools, herndon, development

Article Text

Fairfax County Public Schools is heading into a pivotal budget vote with encouraging news about employee retention, student innovation and community support across the division.

Among school-based employees who completed the district’s Intent to Return Form, 97% said they plan to return for the 2026-27 school year. FCPS received responses from 99% of those staff members, giving the school system valuable information for workforce and instructional planning.

The Fairfax County School Board is scheduled to vote Thursday on the proposed fiscal year 2027 advertised budget. The proposal comes as FCPS works to close a funding gap that could be reduced significantly by additional state support.

More information about possible funding from the Commonwealth is expected around March 14. School officials are continuing discussions with funding partners while inviting the public to follow the budget process and upcoming engagement opportunities.

FCPS also recently received a letter from Sen. Mark Warner recognizing its designation as one of the 2025 Best Communities for Music Education by the National Association of Music Merchants. Warner praised the division’s commitment to funding, staffing and maintaining high standards for music instruction, noting that music can strengthen students’ creativity, confidence, communication and connection to their schools.

The recognition came during a week that also included the district’s playful “FCPS Thaw,” when students and employees wore tropical shirts and bright colors on a cold, rainy Thursday in hopes of ushering out winter.

More Than 1,100 Students Take On AI Challenge

Families and community leaders gathered Tuesday at Herndon High School for the Seize the Moment Student Artificial Intelligence Innovation Challenge.

Organized with the FCPS Department of Information Technology, Amazon and Playlab, the event showcased student-built applications intended to address real-world issues. More than 1,100 students representing every FCPS high school participated, with the top teams from each school presenting their work at the evening event.

Students created their applications with Playlab, a sandboxed AI platform that allows experimentation without giving users access to private data or the open internet. Their projects addressed subjects including mental health services, government policy and the transition experienced by international students.

McLean High School ninth grader Emmanuel Beakal won the grand prize for MyCompass, an application designed to make mental health resources more accessible to fellow students.

Woodson High School student Joelle Rice placed second with Genetic Guard. Her application seeks to help the families of cancer patients understand complicated genetic information through explanations intended to be clear, reassuring and supportive.

Both applications include security protections and tell users they are not replacements for medical professionals. The challenge supports the innovation goal in the FCPS 2023-30 Strategic Plan.

Aging Meal Van Finds a New Role in Auto Shop

On Wednesday, the Auto Tech program at Fairfax High School received a donated van from Fairfax Area Christian Emergency and Transitional Services, known as FACETS.

Teacher John King and his students accepted the keys to the vehicle, which spent roughly 20 years helping FACETS deliver thousands of meals while accumulating thousands of miles. Although the van had reached the end of its practical life on the road, it will now give automotive students hands-on experience in the school’s program.

FACETS provides hot meals, pantry staples and emergency help to vulnerable people in Fairfax County. McKay Chevrolet was also among the community partners supporting the donation.

Student Environmental Council Expands Its Work

The Superintendent’s Youth Environmental Council held its third meeting Wednesday evening, bringing together students from eight high schools.

Participants discussed writing an opinion article about the Potomac River sewage spill, pursuing grants and attracting more students to the council and other environmental efforts.

Students divided into four working groups focused on policy and legislative advocacy, divisionwide environmental events, school awareness programs, and practical school resources such as recycling and food-sharing initiatives.

The council is seeking members from every FCPS high school. Its next meeting is scheduled for March 12 from 5 to 6 p.m. at the Gatehouse Administration Center Café, 8115 Gatehouse Road in Falls Church. Interested students and their families may contact the council by email with the student’s name, school and contact information.

The effort aligns with the strategic plan’s goal of ensuring students feel safe, supported, included and empowered.

Annandale Partnership Highlights Student Opportunities

At a Thursday meeting of the Annandale Rotary Club, FCPS Superintendent Michelle Reid discussed progress toward the district’s 2023-30 Strategic Plan, budget planning, future career paths and the emerging workforce.

The Rotary Club supports Annandale High School through programs that include the Just World Festival, speech and music contests, student leadership opportunities such as the Interact Summit, and projects to improve the campus grounds.

The partnership between the club and the school, led by Principal Shawn De Rose, was highlighted as an example of community organizations helping expand opportunities for students.

The week concluded with several athletic and arts events. Reid attended Friday’s girls’ basketball district championship games between Hayfield Secondary School and Edison High School, and between Oakton and Centreville high schools.

On Saturday, she attended Lake Braddock Secondary School’s production of “Legally Blonde.” The performance showcased students working both onstage and behind the scenes while developing skills in communication, collaboration and problem-solving.
