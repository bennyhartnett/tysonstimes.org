Nearly 1,000 New Fairfax Teachers Launch Their First Year Together

Nearly 1,000 new Fairfax County Public Schools teachers gathered at Hayfield Secondary School on Wednesday, Aug. 6, for a high-energy introduction to the school division and the year ahead.

By Tysons Times Staff

Coverage area
Fairfax

Schools
Published
August 12, 2025
Topics
schools, fairfax, development

Article Text

Nearly 1,000 new Fairfax County Public Schools teachers gathered at Hayfield Secondary School on Wednesday, Aug. 6, for a high-energy introduction to the school division and the year ahead.

The educators attended the 31st annual Great Beginnings Summer Institute, a program that has expanded dramatically since launching in 1995 with 50 participants and two coaches. This year’s group included recent college graduates, career changers, teachers from other districts and educators arriving from abroad.

The four-day institute combines professional development, networking and orientation. Participants spend two days in grade-level or subject-specific groups, allowing them to connect with colleagues working in similar roles. A third day focuses on orientation at their assigned schools, while a divisionwide kickoff brings the new hires together.

At the kickoff, representatives from FCPS offices, unions, the Ombuds Office, Apple Federal Credit Union, FCPS Pride and other organizations offered resources and giveaways. The Oakton High School Cougarettes Dance Team performed as attendees reviewed the newly released 2025-26 Employee Handbook.

The Department of Strategy, Planning, and Learning, Office of Professional Learning and Department of Human Resources collaborated on the event.

Superintendent Dr. Michelle Reid welcomed the teachers and emphasized their role in serving students, families and the wider community.

“We’re so glad you’re here to help us build a brighter future for students, families, and the community,” Reid said. “This is our mission, our calling, and our great privilege. And it’s my great privilege to work alongside you!”

Chief of Human Resources William Solomon also highlighted the influence the educators will have in their classrooms.

“Your strength, knowledge, and passion will be crucial as you nurture, mentor, inspire, and guide our future generations,” Solomon said.

The incoming teachers brought a wide range of experiences and ambitions. Ricardo Rodriguez traveled from Nicaragua through the Participate Learning program and said he hopes both to strengthen his teaching and introduce students to his culture.

Matthew Nicholson, a Marshall High School graduate returning to FCPS as an educator, said he was eager “to be doing the work.”

Cade Clemons previously worked as an instructional assistant in Roanoke. He will teach adapted curriculum history and English/reading at Fairfax High School. Clemons said FCPS’ resources and support attracted him to the division, adding that he is excited to understand his students “through a different lens.”

Support for the new teachers will continue beyond the opening event. Great Beginnings operates throughout the year, while additional cohorts provide mentorship and encouragement to educators in their second and third years with the division.

Solomon closed by telling the group that FCPS had hired an exceptional class of educators.

“Our students need you, and we are incredibly fortunate to have you with us!” he said.

With their first official school day still ahead, the teachers left Hayfield equipped with new contacts, information and resources for the start of their FCPS careers.
