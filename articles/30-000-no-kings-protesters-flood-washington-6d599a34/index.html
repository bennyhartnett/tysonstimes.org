30,000 “No Kings” Protesters Flood Washington

An estimated 30,000 demonstrators marched from Arlington into Washington, D.C., on March 28 as the “No Kings” movement staged coordinated protests at more than 3,100 locations nationwide.

By Tysons Times Staff

Article Text

An estimated 30,000 demonstrators marched from Arlington into Washington, D.C., on March 28 as the “No Kings” movement staged coordinated protests at more than 3,100 locations nationwide.

The Washington event was among the movement’s largest gatherings. Protesters of different ages and backgrounds carried signs, flags, banners and noisemakers criticizing President Donald Trump and what participants described as an expansion of executive power.

Organizers estimated that 9 million people participated across the country, which would make the event the largest coordinated demonstration in U.S. history. Protests took place in major cities including New York, Los Angeles, San Francisco and Chicago, as well as smaller communities.

The capital was already crowded for the National Cherry Blossom Festival and the Blossom Kite Festival near the Washington Monument, bringing additional visitors to the National Mall as demonstrators arrived.

The march began in Arlington at 10 a.m. and crossed Memorial Bridge toward the Lincoln Memorial before continuing onto the National Mall. Organizers said many participants later crossed the Frederick Douglass Bridge and passed Fort McNair.

Participants said they were protesting Trump administration policies, including its approach to immigration and military actions abroad. They argued that those decisions exceeded constitutional limits on presidential authority.

Marcus Lee traveled from Houston to join the Washington march, saying he chose the capital because “this is where people pay attention.”

“These protests are happening back home, but coming here shows how many people across the country feel the same way,” Lee said.

Jacqueline Hughson came from Cleveland after the nationwide scale of the demonstrations influenced her decision to attend.

“It’s one thing to see a protest in one city,” Hughson said. “It’s another when it’s happening everywhere at once—which makes it feel like people are really paying attention.”

Local residents also joined the demonstration. Aaliyah Jones of Northwest Washington said concerns about executive authority and federal policy brought her to the march.

“For me, this protest is about modern day accountability,” Jones said. “A lot of people feel like the [Trump] administration keeps pushing further and further across boundaries that it shouldn’t be able to cross, and frankly there has to be some visible response to that.”

The demonstration wound down gradually between 2 and 6 p.m. No violence was reported. Some visitors stayed in Washington for the weekend’s festivals, while others began their trips home.

“The trip [to Washington] took time, but eventually there comes a point where disagreeing no longer feels like enough,” Hughson said. “For a lot of people, this was that point.”
