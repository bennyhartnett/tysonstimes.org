Woman Found Dead Near Hybla Valley Bus Stop Tops Morning News

Fairfax County police are investigating after a woman was found dead near a Hybla Valley bus stop Monday evening.

By Tysons Times Staff

Coverage area
Arlington
Section
Local
Published
February 24, 2026
Topics
local, arlington, development, public safety

Article Text

Fairfax County police are investigating after a woman was found dead near a Hybla Valley bus stop Monday evening.

Officers discovered the woman with wounds to her upper body near Richmond Highway and Arlington Drive at approximately 7:20 p.m. Police asked anyone with information to call 911.

Virginia Senate Democrats have proposed eliminating a sales tax exemption for data centers to help fund the state budget over the next two fiscal years. A House of Delegates plan calls for more modest spending on key items. Both proposals would address federal funding gaps affecting food assistance, Affordable Care Act subsidies and other priorities.

Repairs to the collapsed Potomac Interceptor are progressing with assistance from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. D.C. Water crews are working toward restoring full flow by mid-March.

Victor Hoskins plans to return to the private sector this fall after six years as president and CEO of the Fairfax County Economic Development Authority. Board chair James Quigley said the authority will conduct an aggressive local search, with a successor expected to be announced later this year.

Virginia Sens. Mark Warner and Tim Kaine voiced support for the National Transportation Safety Board’s final report on the January 2025 midair collision near Reagan National Airport. They said they plan to pursue legislation reducing flight slots at DCA and implementing other NTSB safety recommendations.

A new statewide poll found that most Virginians support stricter gun laws and retaining the commonwealth’s current redistricting system. Many respondents nevertheless questioned whether tighter firearm restrictions would improve their safety, while expressing strong dissatisfaction with the country’s direction.

Pizzato Pizza has opened at Landmark Plaza in Lincolnia. The casual restaurant occupies a former Pizza Hut at 6226 Little River Turnpike and serves varieties including butter chicken, vodka crispy chicken and chicken tikka pizza.

A Virginia Senate committee reshuffle following Sen. Adam Ebbin’s departure for the Spanberger administration removed Sen. Dave Marsden, a Fairfax Democrat, from the Finance and Appropriations Committee. Sen. Bryce Reeves, an Orange Republican, was also dropped from the Commerce and Labor Committee.

George Mason University’s College of Education and Human Development has launched its Future Ready Teachers initiative. The program will offer aspiring educators full scholarships covering tuition and living expenses, along with mentorship, preparation for technology-enabled classrooms and a three-year induction program.

Tuesday’s forecast calls for sunshine, a high near 39 and northwest winds of 7 to 11 mph, with gusts reaching 20 mph. Clouds will increase overnight as temperatures fall to around 32. There is a 30% chance of snow after 1 a.m., changing to a mix of rain and snow after 4 a.m.
