Snow Response, Student Triumphs Mark a Packed Week Across Fairfax Schools

Fairfax County Public Schools entered December with winter weather, concerts, community gatherings, championship celebrations and major discussions about budgets and academic standards.

By Tysons Times Staff

Coverage area
Fairfax
Section
Schools
Published
December 8, 2025
Topics
schools, fairfax, metro, development

Article Text

Fairfax County Public Schools entered December with winter weather, concerts, community gatherings, championship celebrations and major discussions about budgets and academic standards.

Snow-related challenges put facilities, grounds and transportation employees on an early schedule. The weather management command center at the Woodson Complex was operating by 4 a.m., while crews prepared school grounds, facilities and buses for students.

FCPS deployed 46 grounds employees alongside staff from Equipment Repair, Pest Control, the Stock Room and Grounds Management. Sixteen Plant Operations employees also helped coordinate custodial work across facilities. The response was handled entirely by FCPS employees without contractor support. Officials said the final amount of treatment material used would be confirmed later.

During Friday morning visits to Shrevewood, Graham Road and Woodburn elementary schools, Superintendent Michelle Reid and Chief of Schools Dr. Ponce greeted students, employees and families in the snowy conditions. Shrevewood celebrated the 67th day of the school year, while student safety patrol members helped younger children at Woodburn.

FCPS teams capture state championships

Several Fairfax County high school teams closed their seasons with major victories.

The Lake Braddock boys cross-country team swept the Patriot District, Occoquan Region and Class 6 state championships. Robinson’s girls cross-country team repeated as Class 6 state champion, winning by 28 points and placing three runners among the top 15.

Langley won the 2025 VHSL Class 6 field hockey championship after defeating Western Branch 6-0, Independence 2-0 and Madison 1-0. The Saxons finished the season 22-1.

West Springfield earned the 2025 Class 6 volleyball title in its first state-final appearance. After losing the opening two sets against Colgan, the Spartans rallied to win in five and completed a 30-2 season.

Girls wrestling also continued to grow at the second annual Queen of the Ring tournament at Herndon High School. The competition drew 156 athletes, up from 140 the previous year, from schools across Northern Virginia and West Virginia.

Participating schools included Herndon, Stafford, Prince George, Henrico, Meridian, Freedom-South Riding, Morgantown, Annandale, Langley, Robinson, Lightridge, McLean, Woodbridge, James River–Buchanan, Westfield and Chantilly.

Budget season begins as academic changes loom

Reid and the FCPS School Board joined the Fairfax County Board of Supervisors at a Joint Budget Committee meeting Tuesday to review forecasts and prepare for the fiscal 2027 budget cycle.

The proposed FCPS budget is scheduled for presentation on Jan. 22, 2026. The county executive’s advertised budget is expected Feb. 17, 2026.

FCPS highlighted several measures of academic performance during the discussion: seven of Virginia’s top 10 high schools, five of its top 10 elementary schools and one of the five highest-ranked high schools nationally are in the division. FCPS also reported 261 National Merit semifinalists, a 40% increase from the previous year.

At Thursday’s School Board meeting, Reid discussed the possible effects of new Standards of Learning cut scores and proposed revisions to Virginia’s accountability system.

The State Board of Education decided in November that the score changes would start in the 2026-27 school year rather than 2025-26. The standards would be phased in over four years, raising the passing threshold annually. During that transition, a temporary “Approaching” category would count as proficient.

Using spring 2025 results as a baseline, FCPS estimated that full implementation could reduce the math pass rate from 78% to 52% and the reading rate from 79% to 51%.

The state board was expected to continue its work Dec. 10-11, including setting eligibility rules for locally awarded verified credits and expedited SOL retakes.

Potential revisions to the School Performance and Support Framework include extending support for newly arrived multilingual learners from three semesters to five, changing academic-growth measurements and adding history to the framework’s calculations, which would require more SOL testing.

Other proposals would measure high school mastery using all tested students rather than only seniors and expand advanced middle school coursework to include earth science and world geography by eighth grade.

Airport partnership expands career opportunities

FCPS and the Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority launched an expanded Ignite Partnership at West Potomac Academy on Wednesday.

The agreement is intended to bring more work-based learning opportunities to students across all academy programs. MWAA plans to provide greater exposure to high-demand careers, hands-on experiences and clearer connections between classroom instruction and future employment.

That evening, families gathered at Edison High School for a Quad 3 Family Vision Group meeting. Participants discussed expanding access to Algebra 1 by sixth grade, as well as upcoming school construction and renovation projects.

Assistant Superintendent of Instructional Services Dr. Ellen Reilly and Dr. Amy Hunter led the academic discussion. Facilities chief Erik Gordon, Design and Construction Services Director Scott Eschbach and project manager Holli McKinley addressed building plans.

FCPS also reported that 24 Human Resources employees are attending in-person training every two weeks as they pursue Society for Human Resource Management certification. The program is designed to strengthen current practices in areas ranging from teacher recruitment to personnel management.

New leadership and behind-the-scenes operations

Dr. Dustin Wright took the oath of office Monday as superintendent of City of Fairfax Schools. Wright previously worked for FCPS and now leads the city’s four-school district.

Under a School Services Agreement, FCPS supplies curriculum, staffing and transportation. The City of Fairfax owns and oversees the four school buildings and remains responsible for capital improvements, renovations and management of the agreement.

During a strategic-plan presentation Thursday, FCPS also outlined the scale of its warehouse operation. A staff of 50 serves more than 200 schools and offices, completing about 500 deliveries and pickups each week—or roughly 24,000 annually.

The operation uses 13 distribution trucks and nine Pony mail trucks. Distribution vehicles travel more than 200,000 miles annually, while the mail fleet covers 133,200 miles. Its work includes logistics, daily mail service, surplus handling and sustainability efforts, as well as delivering instructional materials such as ladybug larvae to classrooms.

Music, theater and community events fill the week

Students at Carson Middle School performed at a choir concert Tuesday evening before families and community members.

On Friday, residents attended a Community Conversation at Churchill Road Elementary School despite the cold and wintry conditions. Later that night, Falls Church High School staged Between the Lines before a large audience in its new theater.

First-year theater director Madeline Willard said the production encourages teenagers to accept their differences, resist pressure to fit in and tell their own stories.

The Mount Vernon High School PTSA held its annual Holiday Market on Saturday morning. Much of the money raised will support senior-class activities planned for the spring.

The week concluded Sunday evening with the Service of Nine Lessons and Carols at Christ Church in Alexandria. Reid joined students and families as a reader during the Advent program at the historic church, which was built more than 235 years ago and was attended by George Washington.
