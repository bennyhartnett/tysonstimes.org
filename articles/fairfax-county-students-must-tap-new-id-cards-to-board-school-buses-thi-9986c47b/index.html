Fairfax County Students Must Tap New ID Cards to Board School Buses This Fall

Article Text

Fairfax County Public Schools is overhauling its bus system with card readers that will track every student who climbs aboard, launching one of the largest student-tracking transit systems in the nation.

Starting in the 2026-27 school year, students will tap color-coded ID cards as they board and exit buses. Each tap registers with the Office of Transportation, letting officials know exactly which students are on which buses at any moment.

The system goes beyond simple headcounts. Drivers will see turn-by-turn navigation on new mobile tablets, and each student’s information will pop up on screen as they board—immediately flagging whether a child has climbed onto the wrong bus.

What the System Tracks—and What It Doesn’t

School officials emphasized a key distinction: the technology tracks bus locations, not individual students. The district will know that a student tapped onto Bus 47 at 7:32 a.m., but won’t follow that student’s movements beyond the vehicle.

Parents will gain visibility too. Later this fall, the district plans to launch an app with real-time bus locations and notifications when their child boards or exits.

Cards Required, But Forgetting Won’t Strand Anyone

Every student will receive an ID card, even those who don’t typically ride buses. The cards are color-coded by school and include a QR code on the back, along with the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline—a feature Virginia law now requires on student IDs.

Students who forget their cards won’t be left at the curb. Drivers can manually check students in and out using their tablets.

The ID cards will eventually expand beyond buses, with planned integration for school meals and field trips. Families with questions can contact the Fairfax County Public Schools Office of Transportation.
