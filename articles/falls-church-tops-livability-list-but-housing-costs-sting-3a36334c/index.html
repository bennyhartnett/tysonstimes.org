Falls Church Tops Livability List, but Housing Costs Sting

Article Text

Falls Church has been named the most livable community in the South, earning praise for its healthcare access, parks, walkability and close-knit atmosphere — even as steep housing costs remain a major weakness.

Mayor Letty Hardi highlighted the designation in her June 5 constituent newsletter. The ranking was based on AARP’s Livability Index, which evaluates housing, neighborhoods, transportation, the environment, health, engagement and opportunity.

Falls Church received an overall score of 72 out of 100, the highest among Southern communities. The city earned particularly strong marks for health services, transportation and neighborhoods.

All of the city’s roughly 15,000 residents live within a half-mile of a park or within a mile of recreational facilities. Falls Church also has broad access to professional healthcare services and an estimated smoking rate of 7.2 percent, compared with 16.6 percent nationally.

Its transportation rating reflected transit frequency, walkability and crash rates, along with the city’s connection to the Washington-area Metro system. Falls Church received a neighborhood score of 75, aided by its tree-lined streets and public schools.

Hardi said the recognition reflects years of work by residents, city employees, volunteers, businesses and community organizations. She noted that parks, trails, schools, small businesses and civic participation all contribute to the city’s quality of life.

Housing remains the glaring weakness

The same assessment identified housing affordability and income inequality as serious concerns.

Monthly housing costs were listed at $3,008 in Falls Church, compared with $943 nationally. Residents were estimated to spend 25.3 percent of household income on housing, about 10 percentage points above the national median.

The median home value was reported at $789,604, with prices exceeding $1 million in many neighborhoods. The assessment also found that Falls Church lacked local policies focused on foreclosure prevention and protection or the creation of age-friendly communities.

Hardi said the city must continue creating housing opportunities for people across a range of income levels while preserving the qualities behind its high livability score.

Simon pushes constitutional amendments and criticizes veto

Virginia Del. Marcus Simon has used recent constituent messages to outline priorities ranging from constitutional rights and gun policy to housing, education and collective bargaining.

In a June 8 message, Simon urged Virginians to support November constitutional amendments concerning reproductive freedom, restoration of voting rights for people who have completed their sentences and removal of Virginia’s same-sex marriage ban.

Simon focused specifically on LGBTQ+ rights in a June 2 communication. Although Virginia’s constitutional prohibition on same-sex marriage is currently unenforceable because of court rulings, he warned it could regain legal force if those precedents were overturned. He called for voters to remove the language from the state constitution.

On May 26, Simon listed four broader priorities:

  • Stronger gun-violence prevention measures, including enhanced background checks and additional restrictions on access to firearms.
  • More affordable housing and fewer barriers to development near public transit.
  • Expanded protections for student borrowers and full funding for public schools and colleges.
  • Continued investment in solar power, wind energy and energy-efficiency programs.

Simon also criticized Gov. Abigail Spanberger’s veto of collective-bargaining legislation in a May 15 message. He argued that collective bargaining gives workers greater influence in the workplace and a better opportunity for economic security. Simon said he would continue watching other bills still awaiting action by the governor.

Public comment continues on proposed 250-foot arch

Residents still have an opportunity to comment on a proposed 250-foot “Triumphal Arch” at Memorial Circle, near the Virginia end of Arlington Memorial Bridge.

President Donald Trump has proposed placing the monument within the George Washington Memorial Parkway in Washington, D.C. The proposed structure would stand roughly half as tall as the Washington Monument and above the height of the U.S. Capitol.

Critics contend that an arch of that scale could overwhelm nearby monuments and interrupt the planned visual connection between Arlington National Cemetery and the Lincoln Memorial. Other objections include possible aviation concerns near Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport, the project’s classical imperial style and questions about congressional authorization and funding.

Opponents have also raised concerns that Trump-appointed members of the U.S. Commission of Fine Arts could approve the project without sufficient independent review.

British design critic Stephen Bayley attacked the concept in February, describing it as an outgrowth of Trump’s preference for oversized classical architecture, gold ornamentation and conspicuous displays of status. Bayley compared the proposal with the Arc de Triomphe in Paris and argued that a monumental classical design would be a poor fit for Washington.

Bruce Redman Becker, a former member of the U.S. Commission of Fine Arts, called the proposal architecturally weak and harmful to the historic view toward Arlington National Cemetery. Becker said the commission on which he served would not have approved it. He also claimed that an overwhelming majority of previously submitted public comments opposed the project.

Falls Church resident Don Foley recently encouraged residents to participate in the federal review. He advised commenters to identify specific “adverse effects” on historic resources and explain how the proposed structure could conflict with the legal purpose for which the affected park or historic area was established.

The National Park Service is reviewing the project under Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act. Its assessment examines potential effects on historic properties and archaeological resources within the project area.

Creative Cauldron receives $15,000 grant

Creative Cauldron announced June 3 that it received a $15,000 grant from the Shubert Foundation.

The foundation supports nonprofit professional theaters, particularly resident companies that develop and produce new American work rather than primarily presenting productions created elsewhere.

The Falls Church arts organization, founded by Artistic Director Laura Connors Hull in 2002, operates at 127 E. Broad St. It recently completed a move into a new theater at the Broad and Washington development.

Creative Cauldron began a $500,000 capacity campaign in 2023 to support that transition. The organization ultimately raised approximately $725,000 over two years. Its new venue is expected to provide a permanent home for the next 30 years and serve as an anchor for the city’s planned arts and entertainment district.

Virginia Dream captures regular-season crown

Virginia Dream FC secured the UPSL Virginia North regular-season championship with a dominant 6-0 victory over North Stars FC.

The Falls Church-based club also earned home-field advantage for the postseason and a bye through the first playoff round. Virginia Dream will host a semifinal on Sunday, June 21.

Liam Emson recorded a hat trick in the title-clinching match. Diarra Zoumana, a contender for the conference Golden Boot, scored another goal, while AP Slavov and Ryan O’Neil added one apiece.

The championship joins a growing list of results for Virginia Dream, including:

  • A victory over professional club Richmond Kickers in the 2025 Lamar Hunt U.S. Open Cup.
  • Qualification for the 2025 U.S. Open Cup.
  • The 2024 NPSL Mid-Atlantic Conference championship.
  • The spring 2024 UPSL DMV South playoff championship.
  • UPSL DMV South regular-season championships in fall 2023, spring 2024 and spring 2025.
  • Participation in the 2023 TST $1 Million Tournament.
  • Five VSSL regular-season championships from spring 2023 through spring 2025.
  • Four VSSL playoff championships from spring 2023 through fall 2024.
  • The fall 2022 Washington Premier League championship.

In separate NPSL play, Virginia Dream defeated DMV Elite FC 2-0 behind goals from Emson and Demetrius Shepard-Lewis. The result moved the club to two wins and one loss early in the Mid-Atlantic Conference season.

Virginia Dream’s next NPSL match is scheduled for 8 p.m. Sunday, June 14, against FC Frederick at Meridian High School Stadium, known as “The Cloud.”

Founded in 2022, Virginia Dream is a semi-professional club that covers player costs as part of its effort to remove financial barriers to high-level soccer.

Little City Sips will take over downtown Falls Church from 5 to 10 p.m. Saturday, June 13.

Adults 21 and older may purchase drinks served in official event cups and carry them while visiting businesses and gathering places within the designated event area. The sip-and-stroll will also feature local shops, public art and live music, including a band at Mr. Brown’s Park.

Route 29 transportation study seeks feedback

The Fairfax County Department of Transportation is studying walking, bicycling and other active-transportation conditions along Route 29.

The study covers a two-mile section extending from Interstate 495 to Tripps Run at the Fairfax County-Falls Church boundary, just east of South Maple Avenue. The corridor sits between Interstate 66 and U.S. 50, two routes designed more heavily around automobile traffic.

The county is accepting public feedback and planning a community meeting on the study.

Quantico event connects military families with employers

Transitioning service members, veterans and military spouses are invited to a HIRE VETS NOW hiring and networking event from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m. Friday, June 12.

The event will take place at The Clubs at Quantico and Crossroads Events Center, 3017 Russell Road in Quantico. Base access is required.

Participating employers include Peraton, CACI, Inova Health, Stanley Martin Homes, the FBI, the City of Fredericksburg, SimVentions and the U.S. Secret Service. Available career fields include defense, cybersecurity, healthcare, public safety, government, construction, transportation and professional services.

The event is free and includes lunch. Walk-ins are welcome, although advance registration is encouraged.

Summer concerts add beer and wine garden

Falls Church’s Concerts in the Park series returns for its 34th year, with free performances scheduled on Thursdays in June and July.

Shows begin at 7 p.m. and generally last 60 to 90 minutes. Attendees may bring picnics and lawn chairs, while Ice Cream Jubilee and The Cheese Cartel will sell refreshments.

The 2026 schedule is:

  • June 11 — Tinner Hill Music Concert with the Bushmaster Band featuring Gary Brown
  • June 18 — The Falls Church Concert Band
  • June 25 — Valeria Stewart
  • July 2 — Big Tow
  • July 9 — Andrew Acosta String Band
  • July 16 — Avenue 66
  • July 23 — Sirsy

The opening concert will celebrate Juneteenth. The June 18 performance will feature classical music, blues and jazz, including “Lift Every Voice and Sing.”

Pending approval of a Virginia Alcoholic Beverage Control permit, attendees 21 and older will be allowed to bring beer and wine into a designated garden. Liquor and alcohol sales will not be permitted.

Anyone drinking must carry a valid state-issued photo ID. Alcohol may be consumed only inside the designated area between 7 p.m. and the end of the concert, expected around 8:30 p.m. Consumption will not be allowed if a concert is canceled and must stop immediately if a performance ends early.

Weather-related cancellations or delays will be announced by 3 p.m. on the day of each concert.
