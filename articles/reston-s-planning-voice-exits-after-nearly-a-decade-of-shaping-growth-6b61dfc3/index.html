Reston's Planning Voice Exits After Nearly a Decade of Shaping Growth

John Carter, the Hunter Mill District Planning Commissioner who helped guide development along the Silver Line corridor including Tysons and Reston, announced his retirement at...

Topics
reston, planning, development

Article Text

John Carter, the Hunter Mill District Planning Commissioner who helped guide development along the Silver Line corridor including Tysons and Reston, announced his retirement at Wednesday’s meeting after almost nine years on the body.

Carter’s departure comes more than three years before his term was set to expire in December 2029. He offered no detailed explanation for stepping down early but said he would remain available through September to ensure a smooth transition. His successor is expected to be appointed at the commission’s next meeting that month.

A Reston resident for more than 40 years, Carter brought unusual depth to the role. Before joining the planning commission in 2017, he served on the county’s Architectural Review Board and the Hunter Mill District Land Use Committee. His professional background includes a 35-year career with the Montgomery County Planning Department in Maryland.

Commission chair Phil Niedzielski-Eichner called Carter his “touchstone” and credited him with building community trust during contentious planning processes.

“Quality and genuine are two characteristics that are easy to use for you,” Niedzielski-Eichner said, noting that the 2023 Reston Comprehensive Plan update won broad community support despite initial anxiety over density and development.

Carter chaired the commission’s urban development and placemaking committee, a role Niedzielski-Eichner called fitting given his leadership on urbanization along the Silver Line corridor.

Fellow commissioners described Carter as both expert and mentor. Braddock District Commissioner Mary Cortina said she has referenced his placemaking brochures so often “they’re kind of getting ratty now.” Sully District Commissioner Evelyn Spain and Mount Vernon Commissioner Walter Clarke both credited Carter with offering guidance and encouragement to newer members.

Hunter Mill District Supervisor Walter Alcorn released a statement praising Carter’s contributions, saying his influence “is visible to everyone who lives or works in Reston and the Hunter Mill District.”

The Hunter Mill District Office will hold a public reception honoring Carter on Tuesday, September 8, from 3 to 5 p.m. at the North County Governmental Center at 1801 Cameron Glen Drive in Reston.
