Boundary Shake-Up Could Move 2,210 Fairfax Students

Proposed boundary changes could affect about 2,210 students at 52 schools, with a vote on the recommendations scheduled for Jan. 22.

By Tysons Times Staff

Coverage area
Fairfax
Section
Schools
Published
January 12, 2026
Topics
schools, fairfax, development

Article Text

Proposed boundary changes could affect about 2,210 students at 52 schools, with a vote on the recommendations scheduled for Jan. 22.

Superintendent Michelle Reid presented the proposal on Thursday following a 16-month review that included data analysis and community engagement under Policy 8130. The estimated impact includes 1,174 elementary students, 447 middle school students and 589 high school students.

Reid spent part of Saturday listening to residents voice support and concerns about the recommendations after attending a General Assembly pre-session public hearing at the Fairfax Government Center.

The boundary debate capped a busy week that also included national science honors, artificial intelligence training and the expansion of a student-led environmental council.

Nine local students were selected as scholars in the 2026 Regeneron Science Talent Search. Eight attend Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology, while one attends Chantilly High School.

They were among 300 scholars chosen from 2,600 entrants. Each student and their school will receive $2,000. Forty finalists were expected to be announced Jan. 21 and invited to Washington, D.C., in March for final judging, a public exhibition and awards that include a $250,000 top prize.

At Robinson Secondary School, the rifle team defeated Wakefield High School 1582.2 to 1447.1 during a Monday match at the Woman’s Club of Arlington. Robinson is one of seven local teams in the 12-team Potomac High School Rifle League and won the league’s 2024-25 regional match. Male and female athletes compete alongside one another in the sport.

Technology support specialists and school-based technology specialists attended a half-day ChatGPT training Tuesday led by OpenAI education lead Sam Kanning-Caplan. The program covered the platform’s capabilities and possible instructional uses, followed by a question-and-answer session with division leaders.

The school system’s AI principles call for teachers to remain responsible for learning and decisions, with the technology serving as an aid rather than a replacement. The approach also emphasizes academic integrity, student privacy, security and continued monitoring for potential benefits and harm.

Reid called the rapid emergence of AI a “Sputnik moment” for education. Plans include reviewing tools through procurement and privacy procedures, expanding employee training and launching an AI Think Tank to guide the technology’s use in classrooms.

Reid also met Tuesday with the Rotary Club of McLean at Redeemer Lutheran Church to discuss progress toward the 2023-30 Strategic Plan, budget planning, future career paths and workforce development.

The club has supported literacy at Timber Lane Elementary School for 17 years through its Love of Reading Program. An annual grant helps purchase books, while club members and community guests read to children from prekindergarten through second grade.

Later Tuesday, Reid toured the Fairfax County Juvenile Detention Center and met employees involved in educational programming and other services for young people who have entered the juvenile justice system.

The Student Youth Environmental Council held its second meeting Wednesday. Created by students, the group brings together participants from across the school division to connect student advocacy with policies and initiatives such as Get2Green.

Its goals include establishing a student-led governance structure, incorporating Mission Green Global toolkits and monthly environmental challenges into existing programs, and giving students a voice in local and statewide environmental policy.

Early priorities include improving recycling, introducing “share tables” to reduce food waste, creating a divisionwide science fair focused on environmental issues and innovations, and supplying families with resources that connect school sustainability efforts to habits at home.

The council has moved to a monthly meeting schedule and is accepting new members. Its next meeting was scheduled for 5 p.m. Jan. 27 at the Gatehouse Administration Center Café, 8115 Gatehouse Road in Falls Church.

On Thursday, Reid also served on the five-member AASA National Blue Ribbon selection panel responsible for choosing the next National Superintendent of the Year.
