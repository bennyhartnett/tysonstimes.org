# Tysons Times brand kit

## Brand idea

Tysons Times is a metropolitan civic newspaper: precise enough for a public-meeting agenda, approachable enough for a neighbor’s morning read. The identity pairs the authority of a traditional masthead with the movement and geometry of Tysons—street grids, turns, towers, and the Silver Line. “The corner” is both a place and a promise: news close to home, plus a useful view of what is coming next.

**Positioning:** Independent local journalism for Tysons and the Silver Line corridor.

**Primary line:** What’s happening around the corner?

**Coverage promise:** Your corner of Northern Virginia, covered.

**Voice:** Clear, local, useful, unshowy. Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs. Explain consequences before process. Never manufacture urgency.

## Message system

The primary line is a real question the newspaper answers every day. Keep the curly apostrophe and question mark in display use. Use sentence case; do not set the full line in all caps.

| Job | Line | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Master tagline | What’s happening around the corner? | Masthead, social cards, launch materials |
| Foresight | See what’s coming around the corner. | Briefings, civic decisions, development coverage |
| Belonging | Your corner of Northern Virginia, covered. | About copy, subscription and support messaging |
| Discovery | Just around the corner. | Events, dining, openings, weekend guides |
| Retention | Never miss what’s around the corner. | Newsletter and follow prompts |
| Community | Every corner has a story. | Profiles, schools, neighborhood features |
| Point of view | The view from our corner. | Columns, explainers, year-in-review packages |
| Campaign sign-off | Your corner. Your Times. | Posters, house ads, compact merchandise |

Use one “corner” line per composition. The wordplay should feel observant, never cute for its own sake. Avoid “cornering” a person or story, which sounds adversarial, and avoid pairing two corner lines in the same module.

## Logo system

- **Primary wordmark:** “Tysons Times” in UnifrakturCook Bold. “Times” uses Civic Blue.
- **TT seal:** A compact square monogram crossed by a diagonal transit line. Use for favicons, compact headers, social avatars, and small brand moments.
- **Clear space:** Keep at least the height of the capital `T` around the wordmark and one quarter of the seal width around the seal.
- **Minimum size:** Wordmark 150 px wide; seal 20 px wide.
- Do not stretch, rotate, outline, add shadows, or recolor individual letters beyond the approved lockup.

## Typography

- **Masthead:** UnifrakturCook Bold. Brand use only.
- **Editorial:** Newsreader, optical sizing enabled. Headlines, decks, pull quotes, and long-form body copy.
- **Utility:** Libre Franklin. Navigation, labels, dates, bylines, forms, and data.
- Use sentence case for headlines. Reserve uppercase with generous tracking for short labels and section markers.

## Graphic language

- Fine one-pixel rules organize information.
- A 45-degree line represents movement through the Tysons street grid and Silver Line corridor.
- An open right-angle “corner” can frame a headline, crop photography, or end a rule. Keep it open and directional rather than box-like.
- Small outlined diamonds mark sections and editorial signals.
- Grid patterns stay subtle: 5–12% opacity, never behind body copy.
- Photography should feel observed rather than staged, with recognizable local context whenever possible.

## Editorial usage

- Lead with what changed and why it matters locally.
- Name the neighborhood, agency, street, school, or station when it adds clarity.
- Prefer “people in Tysons” to vague audience labels.
- Corrections should be direct, dated, and easy to find.
